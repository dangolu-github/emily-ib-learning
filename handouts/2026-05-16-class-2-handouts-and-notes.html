Demonstrating the fixes

Additionally, the fact sheet pairs pictographs and charts with concise numerical language to transform abstract statistics into concrete and memorable facts. At the top of the layout, the pie chart labelled “more than 95%,” rendered in dark blue with bold white sans-serif type, immediately establishes the scale of the problem. The phrase “more than” emphasises the seriousness of the figure, while the almost complete circle visually reinforces its size. As a result, the reader processes the magnitude of the statistic both linguistically and visually, making the danger of fall-related injuries easier to understand and harder to ignore. Beneath this, the pictograph of four human figures, with one shaded in orange, accompanies the ratio “1 out of 4.” The contrast between the highlighted figure and the three uncoloured ones turns an abstract proportion into a relatable human image. While the percentage communicates scale, this icon communicates personal probability, guiding readers from a general statistic towards an individualised sense of risk that they may connect to their own elderly relatives. This pattern of language and image reinforcement continues throughout the fact sheet. The clock icon beside “every 11 seconds” creates urgency by suggesting repeated and ongoing danger, while the eye icon beside “vision problems” uses a recognisable body part to represent a medical risk factor, making the cause of falling clear to a non-expert audience. In each pairing, the visual does not simply decorate the statistic; it helps construct its meaning. Families and caregivers are therefore not required to interpret raw data, but are guided to recognise the danger as personally relevant and preventable. This supports the fact sheet’s purpose of encouraging everyday awareness around fall prevention, turning what could have been lifeless data into a clear reminder of real risk for elderly people and those who care for them.
