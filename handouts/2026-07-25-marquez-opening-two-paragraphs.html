Detailed Analysis of Paragraphs 1 and 2

Class annotation

Paragraph 1: Reality and fantasy

  • Opening method: Pigafetta's account works as a historical anecdote, grounding the speech in documented testimony.
  • Central tension: “Strictly accurate” history contains details that sound invented.
  • Effect: The paradox challenges the audience's assumptions about what counts as believable reality.
  • Wider purpose: Márquez asks a European literary audience to understand Latin America through its own history rather than external expectations.

Paragraph 2: Greed and literary identity

  • Literary identity: “The seeds of our present-day novels” connects colonial chronicles to later Latin American writing.
  • El Dorado: The map changes to fit the fantasy, showing how greed projects desire onto geography.
  • Accumulation: El Dorado, Alvar, the mules, the hens, and the railway build an escalating pattern of colonial obsession.
  • Lexical field: Repeated gold imagery unifies the examples around wealth, extraction, and power.
  • Structural role: The final sentence moves the speech from colonial myth toward later political history.

Build the analytical paragraph

  1. Choose paragraph 1 or paragraph 2 and state one clear analytical angle.
  2. Select a short piece of evidence that directly supports the angle.
  3. Name the technique precisely.
  4. Explain the effect created by the language or structure.
  5. Connect that effect to Márquez's wider purpose and audience.
